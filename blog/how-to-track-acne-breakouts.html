Why your skin lies to you

An individual inflamed spot runs its own course over roughly one to three weeks, treated or not. You almost never start a new product on an average day. You start it on your worst day, when your skin has nowhere to go but back towards its own baseline. Whatever you happened to buy that week collects the credit.

Then there is memory. Ask yourself how bad your chin was six weeks ago and you will not produce a number, you will produce a feeling, and that feeling is shaped almost entirely by what you can see in the mirror right now. Good weeks flatten out. Bad weeks stretch. Neither is a measurement.

Skin is also genuinely personal. A systematic review in JAAD International found the evidence linking dairy to acne is mixed and appears to depend on sex, ethnicity and the surrounding diet, while high glycaemic index and glycaemic load showed a modest but consistent effect. A population average cannot tell you whether milk is a problem for you. Only your own record can do that.

The cream with nothing in it works too

This is the number that should change how you shop.

A 2026 review of placebo-controlled dermatology trials in Skin Health and Disease examined what happened to the vehicle arms, the participants handed the cream base with no active ingredient in it. Across two phase III acne trials covering 2,817 people, the vehicle alone produced up to a 51.2% reduction in inflammatory lesions. In one of them the vehicle cut inflammatory lesions by 50.3% against 53.8% for the real treatment, and the gap between the two was not statistically significant.

Half the spots went, on a cream with nothing in it.

So when a serum halves your breakout, you have learned very little. Roughly half a breakout clears on a plain base and eight weeks of patience. Without a baseline you cannot separate the product from the calendar, and you will keep paying for the calendar.

The other half of the problem is that people abandon the things that might genuinely have worked. Electronic monitoring of topical acne treatment shows adherence sliding from around 85% at the start of a course to about 42% by the end. The American Academy of Dermatology asks for at least four weeks before you judge anything, some visible improvement at four to six weeks, and two to three months for real clearing. Most people have already moved on to the next bottle.

How to track a breakout properly

The method is simple and it is boring, which is why it works. Seven rules.

  1. Photograph today, before you change a thing. Your baseline is the worst day, and the worst day is now. Miss it and you have nothing to compare against.
  2. Fix the conditions. Same room, same time of day, same light source, no makeup, no flash. Window light in the morning beats a bathroom spotlight, which carves shadows that read as texture.
  3. Shoot three angles. Straight on, then a 45 degree turn each way. Jawline and outer cheek breakouts hide almost completely in a front-facing shot.
  4. Count, do not vibe. Pick one zone, say the left cheek, and count active inflamed spots in it. A number you can plot beats "bad today" every time.
  5. Log the inputs. Product and how many days you have used it. Sleep. Cycle day if it applies. Stress. Anything new at all: pillowcase, SPF, protein powder, gym, a fortnight abroad.
  6. Change one thing at a time and hold it for eight weeks. Two new products at once means two suspects and no verdict. Eight weeks is the minimum window in which the answer is trustworthy.
  7. Read the trend at weeks four and eight. Never day to day. Daily comparison is how good treatments get thrown away.

If you have a cycle, log the day. A survey of 400 women published in the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology found 44% experienced premenstrual flares, and a later lesion-count study recorded a 25% premenstrual rise in inflammatory lesions in 63% of participants. Without a cycle day beside each photo, that monthly rhythm looks exactly like a product failing.

Why not just use your camera roll?

You can. Most people start there, and most people give up within a month or two, because a camera roll is a pile rather than a record.

  • The same angle every time. The ghost overlay puts your last photo faintly over the live camera so you line up exactly. Without it, half the difference you think you can see is the angle, the distance or the light. This one matters more than everything below it.
  • One place, in order. Your skin photos sit in a single dated sequence, not scattered between four thousand other pictures and a screenshot of a train ticket.
  • Notes and numbers beside the photo. This is how you learn what worked, what did nothing, and what made things worse. The week you switched to a richer moisturiser. The month the prescription ran out and you never reordered. Ten days on holiday with different water and a different pillow.
  • Time-lapse export. Change you cannot perceive across a day is obvious at speed. Twelve weeks of daily photos playing in four seconds is a far more honest answer than the mirror.
  • PDF export. More on that below, but it turns your record into something you can hand to a clinician.
  • Privacy. Close-up photos of your face at its worst are not things you want auto-syncing to a cloud account or resurfacing in a Memories slideshow at Christmas. WTNS keeps records on the device, behind Face ID if you want them there.

What to expect, week by week

Pin this somewhere. It is the difference between quitting at week three and finding out whether something works.

WhenWhat is normalWhat to record
Weeks 0-2 Nothing readable yet. If you have started a retinoid, an initial flare is common as congestion already forming underneath is pushed to the surface faster. Baseline photos, all three angles, plus your starting spot count.
Weeks 2-4 Existing spots are still clearing on their own schedule. The first honest signal is fewer new ones forming, not the old ones going. Daily photo, daily count. Note irritation, dryness or peeling.
Weeks 4-6 The AAD says a treatment that suits you should show some improvement by now. First proper comparison against baseline. Not against yesterday.
Weeks 6-8 Most of the visible clearing tends to land in this window. Keep the routine identical. Resist adding anything.
Weeks 8-12 The verdict. Clearing continues, or the trend line is flat. Export the time-lapse. Decide on evidence, not on a feeling.
Week 12+ No meaningful change means this is not your answer. Take the PDF to a GP or dermatologist instead of buying bottle number seven.

When to stop tracking and see someone

Tracking is for working out what suits your skin. It is not a substitute for care, and some things need a professional now rather than in eight weeks.

  • Deep, painful lumps under the skin that last for weeks
  • Any spot that leaves a pitted or raised mark once it heals
  • Acne that appears suddenly and severely in adulthood, particularly alongside other new symptoms
  • Skin that is getting steadily worse across three months of consistent treatment
  • Breakouts that are affecting your mood, your sleep or whether you leave the house

Bring the record with you. It makes the appointment better.
